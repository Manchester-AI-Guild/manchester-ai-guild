# Operating model and funding approach

Status: decided by default on 16 September 2026, building on the Council's decisions of 4 and 11 September 2026. Council ratification requested for the meeting on 10 October 2026. Detailed governance lives in the Guild's private operations records; this page is the public summary.

## The three questions in issue #15, answered

### 1. Does the Guild act as a channel for public funding, grant support or collaborative commissioning?

**Yes, where appropriate.** The Guild is forming as a Community Interest Company (Council decision, 4 September 2026). A CIC can receive grants and sponsorship, charge for services and employ people, while an asset lock keeps surplus for community benefit. The Guild may therefore apply for, receive and channel public funding, grant support or collaborative commissioning so that trusted members deliver services to SMEs, with the Guild remaining rooted in its social mission. Every such arrangement is recorded and reported to members; the Guild does not take a cut of member engagements during its first review period.

### 2. How are members supported when they deliver work to SMEs?

- **Paid products the Guild sells and members deliver:** the onPrem Workshop (1-day and 3-day formats), Direct Match, and readiness assessments. Standard workshop prices are published; a founding discount applies to the first ten bookings.
- **Fair allocation:** opportunities that come through the Guild are allocated by a skills-match shortlist of three, with the business choosing, under a written workflow with conflict-of-interest rules and a quarterly allocation summary to members.
- **Funded delivery:** where the Guild channels grant or public funding (question 1), that funding pays members who deliver to SMEs that could not otherwise afford support.
- **Standing:** Practitioner and Master status on the Individual pathway, recognition in Guild publications with consent, and the Guild as a point of comeback for the SME, which protects both the SME and the member's credibility.

### 3. What is the balance between social mission and a workable commercial model?

**Commercial engagements fund the mission.** The Council minuted on 11 September 2026 that higher-value corporate engagements build a financial reserve that supports the wider not-for-profit mission, including practical AI support that SMEs can afford. In the CIC declaration, any surplus is reinvested in the community purpose: subsidised or free AI support for SMEs and community organisations, education and mentoring, shared resources and standards, events, research and local capability-building. No surplus is distributed for private gain.

## What this looks like in practice

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Legal vehicle | Community Interest Company (registration in progress) |
| Community served | Businesses, entrepreneurs, professionals, educational institutions and community organisations in Greater Manchester and the wider North West, particularly SMEs |
| Revenue lines | Membership tiers (Community tier GBP 30/year; others to be set); workshops and training; readiness assessments; SME storytelling features and nights; publications; sponsorship; research collaborations; the Guild Treasures programme |
| Use of surplus | Reinvested in SME support and the community purpose (asset lock) |
| Member delivery | Skills-match shortlist allocation; no Guild referral fee in the first review period; conflicts of interest declared |
| Oversight | Guild Council; a Treasurer is being recruited; governance, risk and data policies are versioned in the operations records |

## Who can change this

The Guild Council, at any Council meeting. This page is updated when it does.
